# Agent-written projects: results

This study asks what marks an interface that a coding agent wrote (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and the like), as distinct from a page an AI site builder published and from professionally made work. It ran on 2026-11-12. The numbers below are generated by the scripts in this folder.

## What was found

- **Sprawl marks agent-written pages.** Four counts carry it: pairs of text sizes 1 px apart, tracked uppercase labels, the share of text under 12 px, and font weights. Three or more are over their limit on 15.7% of 236 agent-written pages and 3.6% of 277 reference pages. Reference developer-tool sites, the closest kind, reach it 7.7% of the time (3 of 39 pages).
- **The first sprawl set was weaker.** It counted font families, distinct text sizes, saturated hues, labels and small text. On the capture of all 565 reference, 273 builder and 419 agent-written pages, three or more were over on 1.9%, 1.8% and 9.1%. Font families and saturated hues turned out to be as common on reference pages, so they left the set and are now reported as counts.
- **Agents skip the type scale, not the heading structure.** More than four pairs of sizes 1 px apart appear on 39% of agent-written pages and 13% of reference pages. Reference pages skip a heading level, set a lower heading larger than a higher one and mix heading styles more often than agent-written pages do, so none of those is a rule.
- **By the skill's two measured anchors together** (five or more stock defaults, or three or more sprawl measures), `measure.js` rates 33% of agent-written pages and 7% of reference pages Grimy before any judged tell is weighed.
- **The builder cluster reaches a quarter of them.** 24% of agent-written landing pages carry five or more of the nine stock defaults, against 2% of reference landing pages and 65% of builder landing pages.
- **The source shows it before the page renders.** Three or more of eight source variation measures are over the hand-made limit in 56% of agent-written repos, 55% of builder repos, 4% of hand-made repos today and 4% of hand-made repos frozen at the end of 2022.
- **The detector's line rules do not separate agent-written code from hand-made code.** Most fire as often, or more often, in hand-made repos, which are larger. They stay candidates to confirm on a screen.
- **Some signals are the kind of product, not the author.** Dark pages (67% of agent-written sites, 58% of reference developer-tool sites), glass, nested cards and Geist are as common on professional developer tools.
- **Three rules failed calibration.** A limit of 8 text colors is exceeded by 29% of reference pages, so the limit is now 14. A rule of one heading style per level is failed by 56% of reference pages. The worst-case content test breaks a box on 72% of reference pages and 69% of agent-written pages at desktop width, so a break says nothing about who made the page.

## Method

1. **Finding agent-written repos.** `find-agent-repos.mjs` searched GitHub by topic, by phrase and for `CLAUDE.md` files, and took the repos linked from the top posts of 30 subreddits (8,476 posts, read on 2026-11-12). A repo was kept only when it was created in 2025 or later, is not a fork, holds five or more markup or style files, and carries a hard sign: an agent signature on 30% or more of its last 100 commits, or an agent instruction file. Of about 2,100 candidates, about 790 passed. The bench uses 313: every repo with commit signatures, plus Reddit repos with an agent file.
2. **Rendered pages.** `capture.mjs` loaded the homepage each kept repo lists (494 sites, 419 loaded and were not blocked) and measured them exactly as it measured the reference and builder pages. `compare-pages.mjs` compares the three groups.
3. **The skill's own scripts.** `capture.mjs --scripts` ran `measure.js` and `stress.js` on every second reference site and on 280 agent-written sites, once to find the faults and again after they were fixed. The tables below are from the second run. `compare-measure.mjs` compares them.
4. **Source.** `bench.mjs` fetched the source files of each repo in `bench-repos.csv` and ran the detector. Hand-made repos are read twice: as they stand today, and at their last commit of 2022, before coding agents existed.

## Limits

- These are associations in samples. They do not prove cause.
- Most agent-written sites are developer tools, and their authors chose to publish them. The reference set covers many kinds of product. Where a difference could come from the kind of product, the comparison with reference developer-tool sites is given.
- The agent label rests on commit signatures and instruction files. Claude Code signs its commits and Cursor mostly does not, so the signed group leans toward Claude Code. A repo with an agent file may still hold hand-written code.
- The source variation limits are the 90th percentile of the same hand-made repos they are compared with, so the hand-made columns sit near 10% by construction. The agent and builder columns are the finding.
- Hand-made repos are several times larger than agent-written ones (a median of 626 interface files against 48). Rule tables give "fires at least once", which grows with size. The source variation measures are densities and do not.
- The source variation measures need 3,000 lines of interface code. Below 10,000 lines the hand-made baseline is thin (38 snapshots), and a small codebase reaches a density with a single occurrence. The limits were set before the 3,000-line floor was added.
- A homepage is one screen. Sprawl inside an app, behind a sign-in, is not measured here.

## Trial audits

After the measurements, three fresh agents each ran the skill's audit mode on one agent-written site, chosen from the data as heavy, middling and clean. Each reported the rating, and every place the instructions or scripts failed them.

| Site | Predicted from the data | Rating by the skill | The auditor's own judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| nodeterm.dev | 5 stock defaults, 1 of 5 sprawl measures over | Grimy | Grimy |
| crossbeam-permits.com | 4 stock defaults, 3 of 5 sprawl measures over | Grimy | Streaky |
| devspace.run | 0 stock defaults, 0 of 5 sprawl measures over | Streaky | Streaky |

What the trials changed:

- `measure.js` read text color without its transparency and returned 0% to 1% low-contrast text where 2.2:1 to 2.5:1 text was on screen. It now blends translucent text with its ground and skips fades that are still playing. Its readings on the three sites match the auditors' hand checks (8.9%, 2.3% and 4.8%).
- Text inside drawings counted toward the sprawl measures. `measure.js` now leaves out text inside `svg`, and the auditor rejects a measure that is over only because of an embedded demo or a code sample.
- The stock-default anchors fired on a 31-word sign-in screen. They now apply to screens of 250 words or more, the range the evidence covers.
- One cause named by two UX laws was counted as two tells, enough to cross the Grimy line. One cause now counts once. The Fitts's Law and Von Restorff thresholds were narrowed, because as written nearly every professional page failed them.
- The source variation measures flagged a small, well-made site: 5 of 8 over in 2,200 lines, with no sprawl on the rendered page. The floor rose from 1,000 to 3,000 lines and the code-only Grimy anchor from 4 to 5 over.
- The worst-case content test cost one auditor 75 entries to find 1 worth reporting. It no longer rewrites headings, and the audit lists only the breaks on text that comes from data.
- Two of the three checkouts did not match the live site. Setup now checks that the folder serves the URL.

The trials ran on the first sprawl set. With the four measures now in use, `measure.js` reads nodeterm.dev at 3 of 4 over, crossbeam-permits.com at 4 of 4 and devspace.run at 1 of 4.
